\title[SCC-Local Heuristics for MWFAS on Sparse Digraphs]{SCC-Local Destroy-and-Repair Heuristics for Minimum Weighted Feedback Arc Set on Sparse Digraphs}

\author*[1]{\fnm{Soroush} \sur{Vahidi}\orcid{https://orcid.org/0000-0003-1934-6282}}\email{sv96@njit.edu}

\affil*[1]{\orgdiv{Department of Computer Science}, \orgname{New Jersey Institute of Technology}, \orgaddress{\street{Newark}, \postcode{07102}, \state{NJ}, \country{USA}}}

\abstract{%
The minimum weighted feedback arc set (MWFAS) problem asks for a minimum-weight set of arcs whose removal makes a nonnegative weighted directed graph acyclic, equivalently for a vertex ordering that minimizes total backward arc weight on sparse general digraphs. Despite its NP-hardness, scalable heuristics are needed where exact solvers are too expensive and dense tournament methods are structurally mismatched.

We introduce IPSNS (incumbent-protected SCC neighborhood search), an SCC-local destroy-and-repair heuristic that scores strongly connected components by backward contribution, applies weighted top-\(K\) neighborhood selection, performs two-sided arc perturbations, repairs within fixed original-graph SCC neighborhoods, and accepts moves only under strict incumbent protection. IPSNS is evaluated within an integrated framework that also uses a refined LR-TA seed rooted in the Demetrescu--Finocchi local-ratio lineage and an engineered WMSF-style seed derived from Cavallaro and Cutello's minimal-and-stable weighted feedback arc set method.

The paper is a computational optimization and algorithm-engineering study rather than a new approximation-ratio theorem. On the evaluated sparse nonnegative weighted-digraph benchmark, IPSNS attains the best observed backward weight among the evaluated methods on 96 of 97 standard instances. A confirmatory repeated-run study on the 93-instance common sparse subset (20 IPSNS seeds and 20 DRMacIver/FAS repetitions) compares per-instance medians and corroborates the IPSNS advantage (38 wins, 55 ties, 0 losses). Exact validation on 57 small instances matches the bitmask dynamic-programming optimum on 56 cases; time-capped mixed-integer validation on selected medium instances provides additional certified reference points. A dense LOLIB transfer study identifies a scope boundary where matrix-based pairwise-ordering methods are stronger. Code, configurations, committed summary outputs, and reproduction scripts accompany the paper as Online Resource~1 (supplementary PDF and artifact archive; see Data Availability).
}
