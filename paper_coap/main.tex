\title[IPSNS for MWFAS on Sparse Digraphs]{IPSNS for Minimum Weighted Feedback Arc Set on Sparse Digraphs}

\author*[1]{\fnm{Soroush} \sur{Vahidi}\orcid{https://orcid.org/0000-0003-1934-6282}}\email{sv96@njit.edu}

\affil*[1]{\orgdiv{Department of Computer Science}, \orgname{New Jersey Institute of Technology}, \orgaddress{\street{Newark}, \postcode{07102}, \state{NJ}, \country{USA}}}

\abstract{%
The minimum weighted feedback arc set problem seeks a minimum-weight arc set whose removal makes a directed graph acyclic, or equivalently a vertex ordering minimizing backward-arc weight. We study sparse nonnegative weighted digraphs, where exact methods do not scale broadly and dense matrix-based ordering heuristics are not always structurally aligned.

We introduce IPSNS (incumbent-protected SCC neighborhood search), an SCC-local destroy-and-repair heuristic centered on strongly connected components (SCCs) that still contribute backward weight after constructive seeding. IPSNS starts from the better of two attributed seeds: a local-ratio topological add-back construction derived from prior local-ratio work and a WMSF-style weighted minimal/stable construction adapted from Cavallaro and Cutello. It scores SCCs by incumbent backward contribution, perturbs fixed SCC neighborhoods, repairs them with SCC-restricted local-ratio reduction and heavy-first add-back, and accepts a candidate only under strict global improvement. The supporting analysis establishes feasibility, add-back minimality, and incumbent non-worsening, but no new approximation ratio.

On the primary sparse benchmark, IPSNS attains the minimum observed backward weight among the evaluated methods on 96 of 97 standard nonnegative instances. On the 57-instance exact-validation subset, it matches the exact dynamic-programming optimum on 56 instances. In a repeated-run comparison on the 93-instance common sparse subset, per-instance medians over 20 runs give 38 wins, 55 ties, and 0 losses versus the evaluated DRMacIver/FAS executable. A dense LOLIB transfer test marks the scope boundary: matrix-based ordering methods remain preferable on complete weighted ordering instances.
}
