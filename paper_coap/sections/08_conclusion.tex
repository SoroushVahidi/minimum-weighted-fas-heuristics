\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This paper studied the minimum weighted feedback arc set problem through its ordering interpretation on nonnegative sparse weighted directed graphs. The primary contribution is IPSNS, an SCC-local destroy-and-repair heuristic with strict incumbent protection, evaluated within a computational optimization and algorithm-engineering framework rather than as a new approximation-ratio theory. Supporting seeds include a refined LR-TA construction rooted in the Demetrescu--Finocchi local-ratio lineage and earlier author work, and an engineered WMSF-style seed derived from Cavallaro and Cutello \cite{CC25}.

On the evaluated sparse standard benchmark, IPSNS attains the best observed backward weight among the evaluated methods on 96 of 97 instances and never worsens the better internal seed on the full internal benchmark. Exact validation on small instances and time-capped mixed-integer validation on selected medium instances support near-certification on those subsets without claiming global optimality on the full sparse suite.

The ablation study shows that topological add-back is the dominant engineering element among the tested components, with SCC-local refinement providing a smaller additional gain on the ablation subset. The dense LOLIB transfer test defines an important boundary: matrix-based pairwise-ordering methods are stronger on complete dense ordering instances, clarifying that the method is strongest in the sparse weighted-digraph regime studied here.

The practical value of the framework lies in combining observed sparse-benchmark quality, SCC-focused search, and traceable incumbent protection. Within the stated boundaries, engineered seeding plus incumbent-protected SCC refinement is an effective strategy for sparse weighted directed ordering problems. Online Resource~1 provides code, proofs, extended results, and reproduction materials as described in the Data Availability statement.

EXP10 corroborates the sparse IPSNS advantage on the 93-instance common subset: per-instance medians over 20 runs yield 38 wins, 55 ties, and 0 losses versus DRMacIver/FAS, with unchanged 21.6\% mean relative excess.

Several directions follow from those boundaries: stronger dense-native seeding paired with the same incumbent-protection discipline; broader sparse benchmark families; parallel SCC refinement; and sharper medium-scale exact reference through additional certified polishing.
