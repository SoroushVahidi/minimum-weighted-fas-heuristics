\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

The minimum weighted feedback arc set (MWFAS) problem asks for a minimum-weight set of arcs whose removal makes a nonnegative weighted directed graph acyclic, equivalently for a vertex ordering that minimizes the total weight of arcs directed backward with respect to that order. The problem is NP-hard in general \cite{K72} and arises across graph optimization, combinatorial ranking, and algorithm engineering wherever pairwise directed relations are cyclic. A directed graph may encode precedence in scheduling, dependencies in design workflows, or directed comparisons in aggregated data. Rank aggregation from inconsistent pairwise information is one motivation for ordering heuristics \cite{ACN08}, but the computational study here targets \emph{sparse general weighted digraphs} rather than dense complete pairwise-comparison matrices. When arcs carry weights, backward constraints are not interchangeable: violating a high-weight precedence relation costs more than violating a weak one, and an effective heuristic must respect that asymmetry.

Weighted variants of MWFAS have an extensive heuristic and exact-method literature \cite{F90,BSNA21}. Exact methods are practical only for small instances or special structures. Naive score heuristics can be fast but ignore local cyclic structure; random multistart heuristics do not exploit graph decomposition; and specialized dense-ordering solvers may perform well on complete pairwise-comparison instances while being poorly aligned with sparse, irregular directed graphs. A meaningful structural distinction separates sparse general digraphs, where strongly connected components are scattered across a mostly acyclic background, from dense linear-ordering or tournament instances, where every ordered pair carries evidence and the problem is globally coupled. The computational question addressed here is whether an SCC-local destroy-and-repair heuristic, supported by complementary constructive seeds, can deliver strong observed performance on nonnegative sparse weighted digraphs while clearly documenting its scope boundaries.

We introduce incumbent-protected SCC neighborhood search (IPSNS) as the primary new algorithmic contribution. IPSNS begins from the better of two internal seeds and iterates SCC-local destroy-and-repair moves on fixed original-graph neighborhoods. Component selection uses backward-weight contribution scoring and weighted random choice among the top-\(K\) eligible strongly connected components; destroy operations reactivate heavy removed internal arcs and remove light active internal arcs; repair applies restricted local-ratio reduction and restricted heavy-first add-back inside the selected component; and the global incumbent is updated only on strict backward-weight improvement. Under nonnegative weights this yields a traceable non-worsening invariant relative to the best seed (Proposition~\ref{prop:ipsns-monotonicity}), which is an algorithmic quality guarantee rather than an approximation-ratio theorem.

The integrated framework combines IPSNS with two supporting seeds that are not claimed as new algorithms in this paper. LR-TA (local-ratio topological add-back) inherits the directed local-ratio cycle-reduction principle of Demetrescu and Finocchi \cite{DF03} and refines it in an engineered sparse-graph implementation with recorded removals, deterministic topological extraction, and original-weight heavy-first add-back; it is further connected to the authors' earlier ranking-oriented MWFAS work \cite{VahidiKoutis2024arxiv}. The WMSF-style seed is an engineered implementation variant of the minimal-and-stable weighted feedback arc set pipeline of Cavallaro and Cutello \cite{CC25}, used as a complementary constructive baseline rather than as a separate literature claim. Section~\ref{sec:related-work} discusses external literature and Section~\ref{subsec:prior-work} states explicitly how the present manuscript extends related author manuscripts and prepared packages.

The computational study is organized around multiple evidence streams on public benchmarks. The main sparse benchmark evaluates weighted directed graph instances from graph-benchmark collections, with negative-weight instances separated from the standard nonnegative comparisons. Supporting studies include component ablation, exact small-instance validation, time-capped mixed-integer validation on selected medium instances, external baseline comparison, iteration-budget and plain local-search controls, a parameter holdout study, an application-facing public ordering example, a dense LOLIB transfer test, and a confirmatory repeated-run stochastic-robustness study (EXP10) for DRMacIver/FAS on the 93-instance common sparse subset.

The results support a deliberately bounded claim on finalized evidence. On 105 sparse benchmark instances, IPSNS has no incumbent violations relative to either internal seed. In the ablation study on a ten-instance subset, the topological add-back phase reduces mean backward weight by 5.9 percent relative to local-ratio without add-back, and IPSNS contributes an additional 0.75 percent mean reduction on that subset. On the exact-validation subset of 57 standard nonnegative instances with \(n \le 20\), IPSNS matches the dynamic-programming optimum on 56 instances, with mean relative gap about 0.0006 percent. On 97 standard nonnegative sparse instances in the external comparison, IPSNS achieves the best observed backward weight among the evaluated methods on 96 instances; the strongest external baseline, DRMacIver/FAS, is about 21.6 percent worse in mean backward weight on the completed sparse comparison subset. These numbers support strong observed performance within the sparse nonnegative weighted-digraph setting studied here, without claiming universal dominance across all FAS regimes.

The dense LOLIB study is reported as a scope boundary rather than as primary evidence. LOLIB instances are complete weighted ordering instances, structurally different from the sparse directed graphs that motivate IPSNS. On 50 dense LOLIB instances, the matrix-based pairwise-ordering baseline DRMacIver/FAS obtains the best observed solution on 45 instances, while IPSNS is best on 5 instances and has about 3.88 percent higher mean backward weight. This outcome is consistent with the structural distinction: SCC-local refinement is useful when sparse cyclic subgraphs provide meaningful neighborhoods, whereas dense complete ordering instances favor matrix-based pairwise-ordering heuristics.

The contributions of this paper are as follows:
\begin{itemize}
    \item We introduce IPSNS, an SCC-local destroy-and-repair heuristic for MWFAS on sparse nonnegative weighted digraphs, combining fixed original-graph SCC neighborhoods, contribution-based SCC scoring, weighted top-\(K\) selection, two-sided weighted destroy operations, SCC-restricted repair, and strict incumbent protection. The novelty lies in this FAS-specific integration rather than in generic LNS, rollback, or SCC decomposition ingredients taken alone.
    \item We present an integrated computational framework that combines IPSNS with a refined LR-TA seed (inherited from the Demetrescu--Finocchi lineage and earlier author work) and an engineered WMSF-style seed derived from Cavallaro and Cutello \cite{CC25}, evaluated as a reproducible sparse-graph pipeline rather than as three independent literature claims.
    \item We report a computational validation program on public benchmarks: sparse external comparison, exact dynamic-programming validation, time-capped MIP validation on selected medium instances, ablation and budget studies, plain local-search controls, parameter holdout analysis, an application case, a dense LOLIB scope boundary, and EXP10 repeated-run robustness for DRMacIver/FAS (20 repetitions per instance on the 93-instance common subset; per-instance median comparison 38/55/0 in favor of IPSNS).
    \item We provide supporting correctness properties---feasibility of LR-TA and add-back, inclusion-minimality of one-pass heavy-first add-back on an acyclic state, and incumbent non-worsening for IPSNS---together with implementation-faithful complexity bounds and a submission-targeted reproducibility package based on code, configurations, and committed summaries.
\end{itemize}
