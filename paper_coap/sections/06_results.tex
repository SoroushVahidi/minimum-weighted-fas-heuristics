\section{Computational results}
\label{sec:results}

The results are organized around the paper's claim hierarchy. The primary question is how IPSNS performs on the standard sparse nonnegative benchmark. The supporting questions are whether that performance survives exact validation, how repeated-run behavior compares with the strongest external executable, what the ablation evidence says about the integrated design, and where the sparse-graph advantage stops transferring.

\subsection{Primary sparse comparison}

Table~\ref{tab:sparse-external-baselines} reports the main comparison on the 97 standard nonnegative sparse instances.

\input{tables/table_sparse_external_baselines}

IPSNS attains the minimum observed BW among the evaluated methods on 96 of the 97 instances. LR-TA and the WMSF-style seed are the strongest internal comparators, but both trail IPSNS in mean BW and best-count frequency. Among the external methods, DRMacIver/FAS is the strongest comparator on this benchmark, and the generator reports a mean relative reduction of 21.61\% for IPSNS under the baseline-normalized convention. The incompletions matter and are kept explicit: DRMacIver/FAS completes 93 of the 97 instances because two DAG inputs are rejected by the wrapper and two large sparse instances time out.

On the full 105-instance sparse benchmark, IPSNS records zero incumbent violations relative to the better seed. The refinement layer therefore improves selectively rather than trading improvements for regressions. LR-TA remains the clear low-latency option, with mean runtime \(0.08\) seconds per instance and mean BW close to IPSNS, but IPSNS is the method that secures the 96-of-97 sparse result.

Single-run paired outcomes against DRMacIver/FAS on the 93 common standard instances are 37 wins, 55 ties, and 1 loss for IPSNS. Because those counts are tie-heavy and the external executable varies across repetitions, the more informative claim comes from the repeated-run analysis in Section~\ref{subsec:exp10-results}. Full sparse paired-test tables for all baselines are moved to Online Resource~1.

\subsection{Exact validation, MIP checks, and holdout confirmation}

Table~\ref{tab:exact-validation} gives the exact small-instance validation results.

\input{tables/table_exact_validation}

IPSNS matches the exact dynamic-programming optimum on 56 of the 57 standard nonnegative instances with \(n\le 20\). LR-TA matches on 55 and the WMSF-style seed on 51. The exact-validation table uses the committed generator's total-weight normalization, so the reported mean gap is \(0.0006\%\) even though the sole miss on \texttt{r20\_60} has a 3-unit BW gap, corresponding to a 0.178\% optimum-normalized gap. The correct interpretation is empirical near-optimality on the validated subset, not a new optimality guarantee for larger graphs.

The time-capped MIP study extends that picture slightly beyond the DP regime. On 15 selected medium instances, HiGHS certifies optimality on 7 and times out on 8. IPSNS matches the certified optimum on 6 of the 7 solved cases; again, the sole mismatch is \texttt{r20\_60}, with IPSNS BW 1688 versus certified MIP optimum 1685 and an optimum-normalized gap of \(0.178\%\). The remaining 8 cases are reported only as incomplete certification attempts. Detailed per-instance MIP results are deferred to Online Resource~1.

The holdout parameter study is intentionally conservative. Across 18 tuning and 25 holdout instances, six IPSNS configurations produced the same holdout aggregate median final BW under the archived five-seed protocol. The paper therefore keeps the default configuration as a reasonable engineering choice rather than claiming that the selected parameters are uniquely optimal.

\subsection{Repeated-run comparison with DRMacIver/FAS}
\label{subsec:exp10-results}

Table~\ref{tab:exp10-stochastic-robustness} summarizes EXP10 on the 93-instance common sparse subset.

\input{tables/table_exp10_stochastic_robustness}

The per-instance median comparison yields 38 wins, 55 ties, and no losses for IPSNS. Thus, the typical common instance is a tie, but the non-tied outcomes are strongly asymmetric in favor of IPSNS, and several large improvements produce a substantial mean reduction relative to DRMacIver/FAS. The median paired relative excess is 0.0\% because ties dominate, while the mean paired relative excess of DRMacIver/FAS over IPSNS remains 21.6\% under the baseline-normalized convention.

The archived summaries report two-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank and sign tests on paired per-instance median differences, both with \(p<0.001\). For the sign test, ties are excluded from the win/loss count, leaving 38 nonzero paired median differences, all in favor of IPSNS. IPSNS shows one distinct BW value on all 93 instances across 20 seeds under the fixed configuration. That is evidence of empirical stability for this configuration, not proof of mathematical determinism. DRMacIver/FAS, by contrast, shows run-to-run objective variation on 40 of the 93 instances, which is precisely why a single execution is not always representative for that executable.

\subsection{Ablation evidence}

The ablation study on the 10-instance representative sparse subset supports two points. First, topological add-back is the dominant seed-side design choice: removing it degrades mean BW by 5.94\% relative to LR-TA. Second, IPSNS provides a further reduction of about \(0.76\%\) after seeding, and the 50- and 400-iteration variants have equal mean BW on this subset. That second gain is smaller, but it is achieved after a strong seed and under strict non-worsening. The full ablation table and secondary controls are moved to Online Resource~1.

\subsection{Dense LOLIB boundary}

Table~\ref{tab:lolib-scope} reports the dense transfer test.

\input{tables/table_lolib_scope}

The dense result draws the regime boundary cleanly. DRMacIver/FAS attains the minimum observed BW on 45 of the 50 LOLIB instances, whereas IPSNS does so on 5. The mean BW gap is 3.88\% in favor of DRMacIver/FAS. IPSNS remains second overall, but the dense ordering regime favors the matrix-based comparator.

This outcome is consistent with the problem structure rather than contradictory to the sparse story. IPSNS is built around sparse weighted-digraph seeding and SCC-local repair. LOLIB consists of complete weighted ordering instances, so the cyclic structure is much less localized and matrix-based pairwise-ordering methods are better aligned with the input model. The LOLIB test therefore serves as an explicit scope boundary for the main sparse-graph claim.
